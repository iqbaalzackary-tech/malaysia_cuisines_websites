Satay

The Soul of Malaysian Street Food

History

Satay's origins trace back to Arab traders who introduced kebab-style grilling to the Malay Archipelago in the 19th century. The Malay adaptation transformed the dish with local spices — lemongrass, turmeric, and galangal — and the now-famous peanut sauce dip. Kajang in Selangor became so synonymous with satay that "satay Kajang" is a household phrase across Malaysia. Today satay is one of Malaysia's most internationally recognised dishes, appearing at every major celebration from Eid to National Day.


Recipe

Ingredients

  • 500g chicken thigh or beef, cubed
  • 2 stalks lemongrass, finely sliced
  • 1 tsp turmeric powder
  • 1 tbsp coriander powder
  • 1 tsp cumin powder
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 200g roasted peanuts, blended
  • 3 tbsp sweet soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp tamarind juice
  • Bamboo skewers, soaked in water

Steps

  1. Blend lemongrass with all spices, sugar and salt into a marinade paste. Coat meat cubes thoroughly.
  2. Thread meat onto bamboo skewers (3-4 pieces each). Marinate for at least 2 hours, ideally overnight.
  3. Grill over charcoal on high heat, turning frequently and basting with oil. Each side takes about 3-4 minutes.
  4. For peanut sauce — combine blended peanuts, sweet soy sauce, tamarind juice, sugar and water in a pot. Simmer until thickened.
  5. Serve skewers with peanut sauce, cucumber chunks, red onion and compressed rice (ketupat).

Fun Facts

  • Kajang in Selangor is so famous for satay that the town has an entire street dedicated to satay stalls!
  • The bamboo skewers must be soaked in water before grilling to prevent them from burning over the charcoal.
  • Satay is one of the few Malaysian dishes that has its own dedicated festival — the Kajang Satay Festival held annually
