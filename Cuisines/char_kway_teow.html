Nasi Lemak

Malaysia's National Dish

History

Char Kway Teow was created by Hokkien and Teochew Chinese immigrants in Penang in the 19th century. Originally it was cheap, calorie-dense street food designed for laborers and dock workers who needed maximum energy at minimum cost.


Recipe

Ingredients

  • 300g flat rice noodles (kway teow)
  • 150g large prawns, peeled
  • 100g blood cockles
  • 2 eggs
  • 100g bean sprouts
  • 3 stalks Chinese chives, chopped
  • 3 tbsp dark soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp light soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp chili paste (sambal)
  • Lard or oil for frying

Steps

  1. Heat wok over blazing high heat until smoking. This is essential for the wok hei (charred aroma).
  2. Add lard or oil. Fry garlic briefly, then add prawns. Cook until just pink.
  3. Add noodles. Pour in dark soy sauce, light soy sauce, and sambal. Toss vigorously.
  4. Push noodles to one side, crack in eggs and scramble lightly before folding into the noodles.
  5. Add cockles, bean sprouts, and chives. Toss for 30 seconds on highest heat. Plate immediately.

Fun Facts

  • The secret to great Char Kway Teow is "wok hei" — the smoky charred flavour only achievable on extremely high heat.
  • Penang Char Kway Teow is famous for using pork lard and blood cockles, making it richer than other versions.
  • In 2024, Penang Char Kway Teow was listed as one of the world's best street foods.
