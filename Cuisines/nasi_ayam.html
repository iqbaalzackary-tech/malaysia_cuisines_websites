Nasi Ayam

Simple, Soulful and Deeply Satisfying

History

Nasi Ayam, known internationally as Hainanese Chicken Rice, was brought to Malaysia by Hainanese immigrants from southern China in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The dish was adapted to suit local tastes — fragrant rice cooked in chicken broth, tender poached chicken, and a trio of sauces including chili, ginger and dark soy. Over time it became deeply embedded in Malaysian hawker culture, enjoyed by all races and found in every corner of the country from mamak stalls to fine dining restaurants.


Recipe

Ingredients

  • 1 whole chicken
  • 2 cups jasmine rice
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 inch ginger, sliced
  • 2 stalks spring onion
  • 1 tbsp sesame oil
  • Salt to taste
  • Chili sauce to serve
  • Ginger sauce to serve
  • Dark soy sauce to serve
  • Cucumber slices to garnish

Steps

  1. Rub chicken with salt and ginger. Poach in boiling water with spring onion for 45 minutes until cooked through.
  2. Remove chicken and immediately submerge in ice water for 10 minutes — this gives the skin its signature silky texture.
  3. Reserve the chicken broth. Fry garlic and ginger in oil until fragrant, then toast the rice briefly before cooking it in the reserved chicken broth.
  4. Brush cooked chicken with sesame oil and dark soy sauce. Chop into pieces.
  5. Serve chicken over fragrant rice with cucumber slices, a bowl of clear soup, and chili, ginger and dark soy dipping sauces on the side.

Fun Facts

  • The secret to silky smooth chicken skin is the ice bath immediately after poaching — it stops the cooking process and tightens the skin instantly.
  • The rice is just as important as the chicken — cooking it in chicken broth with garlic and ginger gives it a rich, aromatic flavor unlike plain rice.
  • Nasi Ayam is one of the few dishes that Malaysia and Singapore both claim as their own — a friendly food rivalry that has lasted decades!
