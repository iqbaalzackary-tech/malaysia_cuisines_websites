Nasi Kerabu

Kelantan's Iconic Blue Rice

History

Nasi Kerabu is the pride of Kelantan and Terengganu on Malaysia's east coast, where Kelantanese Malay culture is most strongly preserved. The dish reflects the coastal region's abundance — fresh herbs from the jungle, fish from the South China Sea, and coconut from every backyard. The stunning blue color comes from butterfly pea flowers (bunga telang), a natural food dye used in Southeast Asian cooking for centuries.


Recipe

Ingredients

  • 2 cups rice
  • 15 butterfly pea flowers (bunga telang)
  • 100g shredded coconut, toasted
  • 200g fried or grilled fish, shredded
  • Fresh herbs (ulam) — mint, daun kesum, long beans
  • 2 hard boiled eggs
  • Sambal belacan to serve
  • Budu (fermented fish sauce) to serve

Steps

  1. Steep butterfly pea flowers in 2 cups of boiling water for 10 minutes. Strain and use this blue liquid to cook your rice.
  2. Cook rice in the blue flower water. The rice will turn a stunning deep blue color as it cooks.
  3. Prepare all accompaniments — toast coconut until golden, fry or grill fish and shred it finely.
  4. Wash and slice all fresh herbs finely. Arrange into a colorful side platter.
  5. Plate the blue rice and arrange all sides around it. Serve with budu, sambal and egg.

Fun Facts

  • The blue color of Nasi Kerabu comes entirely from butterfly pea flowers — no artificial coloring at all!
  • Nasi Kerabu represents the ulam tradition — the Malay practice of eating raw or lightly prepared herbs as part of every meal.
  • The most beautiful place to find Nasi Kerabu is Pasar Siti Khadijah in Kota Bharu — a market run entirely by Kelantanese women.
