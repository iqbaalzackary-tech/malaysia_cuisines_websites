Cendol

Penang's Most Iconic Sweet Escape

History

Cendol is one of Southeast Asia's oldest desserts, with records dating back to the 12th century in Java. Its spread across the region followed trade routes and the movement of people across the archipelago. In Malaysia, cendol became a beloved Peranakan (Nyonya) dessert and a fixture of market life. Gula melaka — raw palm sugar tapped from coconut palms — gives it a deep, almost smoky caramel flavor that refined sugar simply cannot replicate. Penang is fiercely proud of its cendol, claiming the best version uses freshly made strands and coconut milk squeezed on the spot.


Recipe

Ingredients

  • 150g rice flour
  • 2 tbsp green pea flour (hoon kueh)
  • 500ml water
  • 1 tsp pandan paste or extract
  • Pinch of salt
  • 200g gula melaka (palm sugar), chopped
  • 100ml water (for syrup)
  • 400ml coconut milk
  • 1 can red kidney beans, cooked
  • Shaved ice

Steps

  1. Mix rice flour, green pea flour, water, pandan paste and salt in a pot. Cook over medium heat, stirring constantly until thick and gummy.
  2. Press hot dough through a cendol mold or metal colander into a bowl of ice water. The strands will set into little green worms.
  3. Make gula melaka syrup by simmering chopped palm sugar with water until dissolved and slightly thickened.
  4. Prepare coconut milk with a pinch of salt. Chill in the fridge.
  5. Assemble — pile shaved ice in a bowl, add red beans and cendol strands, pour over coconut milk and drizzle generously with palm sugar syrup.

Fun Facts

  • The green color of cendol strands comes entirely from pandan leaves — a natural flavoring and coloring used across Southeast Asian cooking.
  • Penang Road Famous Teochew Cendol in Georgetown has been serving cendol from a pushcart for over 60 years!
  • Cendol was named one of the world's best desserts by multiple international food publications.
