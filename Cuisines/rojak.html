Rojak

The Bold, Funky Salad of the Streets

History

Rojak is a uniquely Malaysian concept — the word itself means "mixture" or "eclectic blend" in Malay, and has come to describe Malaysia's multicultural society. A bowl of rojak contains Malay, Chinese and Indian ingredients all unified in one dish. The Penang variety uses an intensely pungent black prawn paste (hei ko), while the Indian-Muslim pasembur is a variation with fried fritters and a sweeter sauce. Rojak is also metaphorical — Malaysians use the word to describe mixed-language sentences, diverse friend groups, and anything wonderfully jumbled together.


Recipe

Ingredients

  • 1 cup pineapple, cubed
  • 1 cup cucumber, cubed
  • 1 cup jicama (bangkuang), julienned
  • 1 green mango, sliced
  • 2 pieces tofu puff, quartered
  • 100g bean sprouts, blanched
  • 3 tbsp prawn paste (hei ko/petis)
  • 2 tbsp tamarind juice
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp chili paste
  • ½ cup crushed roasted peanuts

Steps

  1. Mix all dressing ingredients together — prawn paste, tamarind juice, sugar and chili paste. Taste and adjust — it should be sweet, sour, salty and funky.
  2. Prepare all fruits and vegetables. Keep everything chunky — the textures are part of the dish.
  3. Toss fruits, vegetables and tofu puff together in the dressing just before serving.
  4. Transfer to a plate or bowl and pile on the crushed peanuts generously.
  5. Serve immediately — rojak doesn't keep well once dressed as the fruits release liquid.

Fun Facts

  • The word "rojak" is used in everyday Malaysian language to describe anything mixed or eclectic — including Manglish, Malaysia's unique mixed language!
  • There are over 10 different versions of rojak across Malaysia — each state has its own unique twist on the dressing and ingredients.
  • The black prawn paste used in Penang rojak (hei ko) is so pungent that it's an acquired taste — but once you love it, you can't stop!
