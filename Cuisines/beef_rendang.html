Beef Rendang

The King of Malaysian Curries

History

Rendang originated with the Minangkabau people of West Sumatra but became deeply embedded in Malaysian culture through Negeri Sembilan, where Minangkabau descendants settled centuries ago. Traditionally, rendang was made for weddings, Eid celebrations, and important ceremonies because the slow cooking preserved the meat for days without refrigeration. In 2011, CNN named rendang one of the world's 50 most delicious foods.


Recipe

Ingredients

  • 1 kg beef chuck, cubed
  • 2 cans (800ml) coconut milk
  • 4 stalks lemongrass, bruised
  • 6 kaffir lime leaves
  • 2 tbsp kerisik (toasted coconut)
  • 1 tbsp palm sugar
  • 10 dried chilies, soaked
  • 5 shallots
  • 4 gloves of garlic
  • 1 inch galangal
  • 1 inch fresh tumeric

Steps

  1. Blend dried chilies, shallots, garlic, galangal and turmeric with a little water into a smooth paste.
  2. Fry the paste in oil over medium heat for 10-15 minutes, stirring constantly until very fragrant and oil separates.
  3. Add beef cubes and stir to coat in the paste. Add coconut milk, lemongrass and kaffir lime leaves.
  4. Bring to a boil then reduce to the lowest heat. Simmer uncovered for 2.5-3 hours, stirring every 15 minutes.
  5. When liquid has almost fully evaporated and beef is dark, add kerisik and palm sugar. Stir fry until rendang is dry and deeply caramelized.

Fun Facts

  • Rendang can last for days without refrigeration because the slow cooking process removes all moisture — it was nature's food preservation!
  • The key ingredient kerisik (toasted grated coconut) is what gives rendang its unique dry, rich texture.
  • CNN named Rendang one of the world's 50 most delicious foods in 2011, making it internationally famous.
