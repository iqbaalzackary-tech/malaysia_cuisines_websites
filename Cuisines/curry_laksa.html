Curry Laksa

Malaysia's Favourite Noodle Soup

History

Curry Laksa traces its roots to the Peranakan (Baba-Nyonya) community — Chinese immigrants who settled in the Malay Peninsula and blended Chinese and Malay culinary traditions into an entirely new cuisine. The rich coconut curry broth became a staple of Malaysian hawker culture, enjoyed from Penang all the way down to Johor.


Recipe

Ingredients

  • 200g rice vermicelli noodles
  • 300g prawns, peeled
  • 400ml coconut milk
  • 500ml prawn stock
  • 3 tbsp laksa paste
  • 2 pieces tofu puff, halved
  • 100g bean sprouts
  • 2 hard boiled eggs, halved
  • Fresh laksa leaves to garnish
  • Sambal belacan to serve

Steps

  1. Soak rice vermicelli in warm water for 10 minutes then drain.
  2. Fry laksa paste in oil over medium heat for 5 minutes until very fragrant.
  3. Add prawn stock and bring to a boil. Add coconut milk and reduce to a simmer.
  4. Add prawns and tofu puffs. Cook prawns for 2-3 minutes until pink.
  5. Arrange noodles in a bowl. Ladle hot broth and toppings over. Garnish with bean sprouts, egg and laksa leaves. Serve with sambal on the side.

Fun Facts

  • Curry Laksa and Assam Laksa are completely different dishes — Curry Laksa uses coconut milk while Assam Laksa uses a sour tamarind fish broth.
  • Every state in Malaysia has its own version of laksa — the debate over which is best never ends!
  • Laksa was named one of the world's 50 most delicious foods by CNN Travel.
